Retain the original opening terminology:

> Vehicular Fog Computing (VFC) enables latency-sensitive Service Function Chain

## III-D: mobility model

> SUMO supplies epoch-sampled position, speed and heading on a two-lane,
> signalized 5-by-5 road grid spanning 2,400 by 1,600 m. Seeded routes contain
> two intermediate waypoints. At most 300 vehicles are simultaneously present;
> vehicles accelerate, queue, turn and depart according to the microscopic
> simulator. Maximum-speed conditions are generated by applying the stated cap
> to both the road network and vehicle type. Realized speeds can be lower due
> to congestion and intersection behavior. Each speed and evaluation seed uses
> a distinct trace, shared across comparison schemes. Training and evaluation
> mobility seeds are disjoint.

Reason: road motion now comes from actual SUMO execution; vehicle population
and speed caps must not be described as constant population or constant speed.
This generated map is a reproducible new benchmark, not the original missing map.

## III-E: coverage and dwell feature

Keep closest-covering-RSU association. Replace the radial predictor with:

For relative position p = X_v − X_n, current velocity u, and radius rho,

```text
T_exit = [-p·u + sqrt((p·u)^2 + ||u||^2 (rho^2 − ||p||^2))] / ||u||^2
T_dwell = min(60 s, max(0, T_exit)).
```

> A stationary covered vehicle receives the clipping bound. The feature is a
> constant-velocity coverage-exit estimate, not a prediction of future turns or
> of nearest-RSU reassociation in overlapping coverage. It uses no future SUMO
> samples. If a vehicle remains in the trace but has no covering RSU, affected
> active services experience a coverage-gap failure; trace departure and normal
> service expiry are recorded separately.

Reason: the new formula handles inward and tangential motion consistently;
using SUMO does not make the local predictor aware of future road maneuvers.

## III-F and resource assumptions

> Wireless access delay remains fixed at 2 ms. The service-delay model comprises
> processing and backhaul propagation and excludes packet-level radio fading,
> retransmission and queueing. Resource feasibility covers abstract CPU capacity
> and reserved backhaul bandwidth. RAM and GPU capacity are outside the model.

Reason: adding SUMO changes vehicle mobility, not the radio/channel simulator
or modeled resource dimensions. Describe these as limitations, not evaluated
extensions. CPU units should not be equated to verified physical cores.

## IV–V: feasibility, migration and learning

> Initial bounded exploration uses Hc=4, Kp=3 and Mmax=32. If it yields no fully
> feasible plan, fallback explores at most 500 loop-free route prefixes. Budget
> exhaustion is distinguished from proven resource infeasibility. The budget
> limits fallback work but does not guarantee a fixed controller response time
> or eliminate false blocking due to unexplored actions.

> Across preliminary construction and fallback combined, at most 4,096 partial
> VNF-assignment trials are examined per decision. This additional work budget
> controls assignment enumeration independently of the route-prefix limit.
> A feasible RETAIN plan is screened before assignment exploration, and valid
> plans already found remain eligible after truncation. Both budgets and their
> exhaustion counters are reported for all schemes. Unexplored feasible or
> better actions may be omitted.

Add the 4,096-trial budget to the revised parameter table. The original Mmax
bound limited returned actions, not the work needed to find them.

> Make-before-break retains source instances while destination CPU, new-only
> service-route capacity and transfer bandwidth are reserved. Migration paths
> are shortest feasible routes in the positive residual-capacity graph. Actual
> transferred payload includes successful, canceled and partially completed
> preparations. The reward uses planned selective-migration cost; the reported
> actual payload metric is separately accumulated during execution.

> Initial-placement and RETAIN transitions are completed at the next
> request-specific decision or terminal event. Migration transitions complete
> when preparation succeeds, is canceled or fails. A 100 ms nominal cutover
> cost is used in feasibility/reward accounting; the implementation does not
> simulate a separate packet-outage interval. Controller execution time is
> measured externally and does not advance physical simulation time.

The updated decision timer includes reference-route computation, candidate
construction, observation preparation and policy selection, including rejected
attempts. Reference-route runtime is reported separately. Post-selection
application, some pre-event state bookkeeping and external signaling remain
outside this timer. This definition differs from the earlier synthetic plots.

Reason: these corrections match the implemented work budget, MBB resource
accounting and delayed RL transitions. Unconditional completeness, packet-level
seamlessness and real-time latency guarantees would exceed the implementation.

## VI-A: setup and baseline reproducibility

> The main protocol uses 600 s scheduler warmup followed by a 3,600 s measurement
> window, sampled at 1 s, after an additional 120 s SUMO traffic pre-roll. Main
> operating points use ten paired evaluation realizations. The model is trained
> for 15 episodes with the Table I architecture on a separate training mobility
> seed pool. Main sweep intervals characterize evaluation variation conditional
> on a frozen trained policy. Independently initialized training replicates are
> used for the extended reward, arrival-model and encoder studies.

> IM and DLAPM are common-model adaptations with latency-trigger ratio 0.90
> and normalized improvement threshold 0.05, respectively. They share a greedy
> feasible initial-placement rule, whereas STG-DDQN learns initial placement and
> subsequent control. All schemes share request sequences, mobility and initial
> resource realizations, and use the same feasibility and migration mechanics.
> Realized resource states may diverge with their selected actions.

Reason: these are the paper-profile thresholds, not the prior synthetic behavior
settings. Baseline identities and selection code remain IM/DLAPM; Graph-DDQN is
an added encoder ablation, not a reproduction of an external published method.

> Continuity satisfaction is computed over admitted services resolved during

> overhead is actual payload observed over the measurement accounting horizon

Reason: measurement definitions and denominator exposure must remain consistent
with the code, particularly with genuine SUMO departures and short validation runs.
